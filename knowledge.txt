The Ocean

The ocean covers more than 70 percent of Earth's surface and is home to millions of living organisms. It contains many different ecosystems, including coral reefs, deep-sea environments, kelp forests, and open water.

Coral reefs are among the most diverse ecosystems on Earth. They provide shelter and food for many species of fish, mollusks, crustaceans, and other marine animals.

The deep ocean is a dark and cold environment where sunlight cannot reach. Some animals living there produce their own light through a process called bioluminescence.

Whales are among the largest animals in the ocean. Blue whales are the largest known animals to have ever lived on Earth.

The ocean also plays an important role in regulating Earth's climate. It absorbs a large amount of the heat produced by the atmosphere and stores carbon dioxide.

Ocean pollution can harm marine ecosystems. Plastic waste, oil spills, chemical pollution, and rising temperatures can negatively affect marine life.

Protecting the ocean is important because humans depend on healthy marine ecosystems for food, climate regulation, transportation, recreation, and many other resources.
